PostgreSQL Join
Algorithm Research

I'm a co-author on a paper implementing a novel adaptive join algorithm inside PostgreSQL's nested loop executor (src/backend/executor/nodeNestloop.c), targeting VLDB, SIGMOD, or AIDB.

Traditional nested loop joins scan entire relations before producing a single result, which makes them impractical on large inputs. OSL (Online Sequential Learning) treats this as a multi-armed bandit: each outer tuple is explored until it accumulates N consecutive failures, tuples are ranked by the resulting reward, and a left cache is drawn by weighted random sampling — with zero-reward tuples collectively reserved a 10% allocation so nothing is starved of exploration.

ROSL extends OSL with an unbiased estimator of the total join count, so the query can report approximate aggregates at fixed intervals instead of waiting for completion. Because tuples are selected with different probabilities at different steps, naive aggregation is biased; ROSL corrects it with Inverse Selection Probability Weighting and stabilizes variance with adaptive weights, yielding a consistent, asymptotically normal estimator — and therefore statistically valid confidence intervals on the running estimate.

Empirically ROSL lands within 8% error of Ripple Join, an established approximate-join baseline, while producing results significantly faster on large datasets.
